# Game On Night — Slide-by-Slide Crib Sheet

Open this on your phone or print it. The left column is the deck, the right column is what you actually say. Don't read it out — glance, then talk.

**Before you start:** deck open on a second screen, Gemini in tab 2, Nano Banana in tab 3, your sample game doc ready, timer running, recording ON.

---

## 0:00 · Recording ON + welcome

> *No slide. Just get control of the room.*

"Okay — we're recording, so if you're eating, finish it now. Everyone cameras ON please, and change your display name to your real name, it's how we mark attendance. I'm [name], Google Student Ambassador for [campus], GID 13597. Over the next hour you're going to build an original video game and pitch it. Nobody needs experience. Let's go."

**Watch for:** the grid filling with faces. If someone's camera is off, name them politely, now. You cannot fix it later.

---

## 0:03 · Slide 3 — Pre-Survey QR

> **Say:** "Before anything else — scan this. It's two minutes, it's the first thing that has to happen, and I can't start until everyone's in. I'll wait."

Then actually wait. Silence. Don't fill it.

**Watch for:** who hasn't scanned. Note names. With 10–15 people you'll see it.

---

## 0:06 · Slide 4 — What is Google Play Arena?

> **Say:** "Quick context. Google Play Arena is India's mobile esports championship, run by Google Play and NODWIN Gaming. Free to enter. Six games, six clubs. Your skill decides how far you go — from this campus to the national stage in Mumbai."

Then the detail that lands: **"MS Dhoni leads one of the clubs."** Say it plainly. It's the fact that gets a reaction.

---

## 0:08 · Slide 5 — The Clubs

> **Say:** "Six clubs you can actually play for — Mahi Clutchers, which is Dhoni's, plus GodLike, S8UL, Orangutan, Revenant XSpark and Apex. You pick one and compete for it. Pro players and creators reach about 180 million people through this thing."

Don't read the whole list twice. Pick the two you think this room will react to.

---

## 0:10 · Slide 6 — The Challenge: Imagine the Impossible

> **Say:** "Here's what you're doing today. Gen Z lives esports — so you're going to build the world you'd actually want to play in. Original game. One goal, one enemy, one deadline. Who are you, what's your mission, and what's standing in your way?"

Then the three steps, in order:
1. **Dream it up with Gemini** — name, tagline, mission, enemy, characters, mode.
2. **Poster it with Nano Banana** — no text in the visual, keep it positive.
3. **Pitch it in 60 seconds** — poster on screen, doc ready, room votes.

---

## 0:13 · Slide 7 — Prizes

> **Say:** "Three things up for grabs: a Google Pixel for the national winner, a Winner's Certificate endorsed by the programme, and a participation certificate for everyone who takes part. Certificates aren't nothing — you can put them on a résumé."

Be careful here. Don't overpromise. Say what the slide says and no more.

---

## 0:15 · Slide 8 — Make Sure You're Set Up

> **Say:** "Four things, now, while you have a minute: Gemini open and signed in, Nano Banana open in a second tab, and — later — you register on Play Arena. Not now, at the end."

**Watch for:** anyone who can't access Gemini. Sort them out during build time, not mid-pitch.

---

## 0:17 · Slide 9 — What is Nano Banana?

> **Say:** "Nano Banana is image generation. You give it text, it gives you a visual. Today your prompt is the game idea Gemini just wrote for you, and your output is the poster of your game world."

Then the three words: **Prompt → Generate → Present.**

---

## 0:19 · Slides 10–11 — Nano Banana tutorial

Point at the tutorial, don't narrate it all. One warning that saves the whole room:

> **Say:** "One rule that matters: **no text in the poster.** No game name, no tagline, no title. Image only. If Nano Banana slips letters in, re-roll it. Entries with text get marked down."

---

## 0:20 · Slide 12 — What You Have to Submit

> **Say:** "Four things per person: the game doc with name and tagline, mission and enemy, characters and mode — plus the poster. And four artefacts to hand in at the end: the Gemini chat link, the Google Doc link, the poster, and the feedback form."

Then the line that matters most:

> **"If it isn't linkable, it didn't happen."**

---

## 0:22 · Slide 13 — Best Practices

Give them four things, not twelve:

> "Start from the mission, not the mechanics. Make it **specific** — 'an action game' means nothing, 'four campus drones race to save a floating library before sunset' is a game. Then stress-test it: ask Gemini what the weakest part is, and fix that before you pitch. And keep it clean — no weapons, no violence."

---

## 0:24 · Slides 14–15 — What a Great Entry Looks Like

**Do not skip this slide.** The blank-page problem is the number one reason rooms freeze.

> **Say:** "Two examples so you know the bar. **VAULT RUNNERS** — 'every campus hides a secret'. Digging for a vault under the library; the enemy is the Archivist, a clockwork guardian who rearranges the shelves to trap you. Puzzles use real campus landmarks. That's specific. **ECHO PROTOCOL** — you're escaping a doomed space station with the one signal that can save home, and the Static is jamming every door on the way out."

Then: "Notice both are specific, and both have an enemy that creates pressure. That's it. That's the bar."

---

## 0:26 · Live demo — Gemini prompt

Share your screen. Type the prompt live, don't paste it. Let them watch an idea appear.

> "I'm going to do this once, badly, on purpose, so you see it's not magic. It's a conversation."

When it answers, do **one** follow-up out loud: *"The enemy is a bit vague, give me three sharper options."* That single move is what separates a good entry from a flat one, and they copy what they see you do.

---

## 0:32 · Slide 16–17 — Go. Build.

> **Say:** "Twenty minutes. Doc first, poster second. Headphones on, I'll come round. If you get stuck, shout. Timer's on screen."

**During build time:**
- Roam. Anyone staring at a blank doc for 3 minutes gets the VAULT RUNNERS example again.
- Check posters for text. Silently. Re-roll before the pitch, not after.
- Nudge people to save their chat link **while** the chat is open.

---

## 0:52 · Pitch + Vote

> **Say:** "Sixty seconds each. Poster up, doc ready. Name and tagline, mission, enemy, why I'd play. I'm timing you and I will clap you off — it's kinder than watching you run out of words."

Rules for the room:
- One question per pitch, from you, not the audience.
- **Strict timer.** A 60-second pitch that runs to 3 minutes means only 4 people present instead of 8.
- Vote out loud: "Would Play" or "Wouldn't Play". Count hands visibly.

---

## 0:57 · Slide 20–21 — Register on Play Arena

> **Say:** "Before you go anywhere: everyone registers on Play Arena. Scan this, sign up, show me the confirmation. It closes on the 29th and if you're not registered, you can't win anything — including the certificate path."

`goo.gle/GooglePlayArena-StudentAmbassadors`

**Watch for:** the "I'll do it later" crowd. There will be four of them. Get them on the link before they leave.

---

## 0:59 · Slide 22 — Feedback form

> **Say:** "Last thing. Feedback form — this is where you attach your Gemini chat link, your doc and your poster. One form per person. If you close this tab without submitting it, you did the work for nothing."

Wait until you've seen the confirmation screen. Then let them go.

---

## 0:59 · Close

> "Group photo — cameras on, all of you in the frame. Smile, this is going in the recap. Recording stops in three, two — thank you, that was genuinely good."

**Then:** stop the recording, and screenshot the gallery with faces visible before anyone drops off.

---

## Post-call, within the hour

- Screenshot the participant grid **before** people leave
- Export the registration confirmations
- Collect the artefact links into one sheet
- Nominate Best Entry — while you still remember who made you laugh
- Verify the faces in the recording match your registration list, one by one

---

## The four things that will cost you the event

1. Recording not running, or cameras off
2. Fewer than 10 people on screen
3. Registered names that don't match faces
4. A participant who never registered on Play Arena — and then you nominate them as Best Entry
