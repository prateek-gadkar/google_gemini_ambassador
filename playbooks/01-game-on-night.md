## What participants produce

Three artefacts, per participant. All three links get collected:

1. **Gemini chat link** — the shared conversation
2. **Google Doc** — the game concept
3. **Nano Banana poster** — no text in the image, no weapons, no violence

### Game concept doc must contain

- Game name (2–3 words)
- Tagline (one line)
- The Final Mission (one goal)
- The enemy
- 3–4 characters
- Game mode: solo / duo / squad / 5v5

## Participant prompt — Gemini

```
You are my game design partner. Help me design an original game for the brief "Imagine the Impossible".

Constraints:
- One goal, one enemy, one deadline
- Multiplayer mode: solo, duo, squad, or 5v5
- Absolutely no guns, weapons or violence. Positive, creative, campus-friendly.

Give me:
1. Game name — 2 to 3 words, memorable
2. Tagline — one line
3. The Final Mission — the goal, in 2 sentences
4. The enemy — who or what, and why they are in the way
5. 3 to 4 characters, with one line of personality each
6. Game mode, and how a single round plays out in 60 seconds
7. Why players would want to jump in

Then write the whole thing as a clean one-page game concept doc with headings, so I can paste it straight into Google Docs.
```

**Follow-ups (this is what makes an entry win):**

```
The enemy is too generic. Give me 3 stranger, more original options that still fit the brief.

Sharpen the Final Mission into one sentence a 19-year-old would repeat to a friend.

Now write the 60-second pitch I would say out loud. First person, energetic, not cringe. Under 150 words.
```

## Participant prompt — Nano Banana

```
Create a bold cinematic poster for an original video game world.

Scene: [one action moment from your game]
Characters: [2-3 characters — silhouette, colour, posture]
Enemy: [the enemy, looming or present]
Setting: [the world — weather, time of day, terrain]
Style: saturated colour, high contrast, poster-art lighting, dramatic depth of field
Composition: deliberate empty space at the top and bottom of the frame

Strict rules:
- NO text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks or typography anywhere in the image
- NO guns, weapons, blades or blood
- Keep it positive, imaginative and campus-friendly
```

> Re-roll until there is genuinely zero text. Letters sneaking into a poster is the most common reason an entry gets marked down.

## Best Entry rubric

These are the four criteria the programme actually evaluates. Score each nominee 1–5.

| Criterion | What you're looking for |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Playability & originality** | Is it a game someone would play? Is the idea actually new? |
| **Mission & enemy strength** | One goal, one enemy, one deadline. Is the enemy a real obstacle, or just set dressing? |
| **Centrality of Gemini** | Did Gemini shape the output, or was it decoration? If you deleted the chat link, would the entry still make sense? |
| **Poster quality** | Positive, campus-friendly, and **zero text in the visual**. |

**Tie-break:** all three artefacts (Gemini chat + Doc + poster) complete and linkable, and the participant is registered on Google Play Arena.

Nominations are manually reviewed. Don't nominate a friend's half-finished entry.

### Reference entries

The official deck shows two examples of what "good" looks like — use them if the room freezes:

- **VAULT RUNNERS** — *"Every campus hides a secret."* Uncover a vault beneath the college library before the semester ends. Enemy: the Archivist, a clockwork guardian who rearranges the shelves to trap you. Duo co-op or solo, 10-minute levels. Puzzles use real campus landmarks.
- **ECHO PROTOCOL** — *"Silence is the loudest signal."* Escape a doomed space station carrying the one signal that can save home. Enemy: the Static, a swarm of rebel robots jamming every door and map. Squad of 4, 12-minute rounds.

Notice what makes them work: **specific**, campus-or-world grounded, and the enemy creates the pressure.
