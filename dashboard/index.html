Copy-paste: participant prompts

Gemini prompt — game concept
Paste into Gemini chat. Tell participants to save the chat link.
You are my game design partner. Help me design an original game for the brief "Imagine the Impossible".

Constraints:
- One goal, one enemy, one deadline
- Multiplayer mode: solo, duo, squad, or 5v5
- Absolutely no guns, weapons or violence. Positive, creative, campus-friendly.

Give me:
1. Game name — 2 to 3 words, memorable
2. Tagline — one line
3. The Final Mission — the goal, in 2 sentences
4. The enemy — who or what, and why they are in the way
5. 3 to 4 characters, with one line of personality each
6. Game mode, and how a single round plays out in 60 seconds
7. Why players would want to jump in

Then write the whole thing as a clean one-page game concept doc with headings, so I can paste it straight into Google Docs.
Gemini follow-ups that make entries win
Rebel robots and city rescues are the default answer. Push.
The enemy works but it's a bit vague. Give me 3 sharper options that feel specific
to a campus or a city — weird, local, memorable. One line on why each works.

Sharpen the Final Mission into one sentence a 19-year-old would repeat to a friend.

Now stress-test it: what's the weakest part of this game? Name the hole, then fix it.

Finally write the 60-second pitch I would say out loud. First person, energetic,
not cringe. Under 150 words.

Give this to participants

Best Entry rubric

Score each nominee 1–5
Nominated winners are manually reviewed. Nominate honestly.
Judge on the four criteria Google actually evaluates:

1. Playability & originality — is it a game someone would play? Is the idea new?
2. Strength of the mission & the enemy — one goal, one enemy, one deadline.

3. Centrality of Gemini — did Gemini shape the output, or was it decoration?

Pillar 3 · Product Understanding · Must do

Study Notebooks: One Student. Three Steps. One Sitting.

Most students haven't seen any of this. Change that, one conversation at a time.
MANDATORY 15 pts / verified participant min 10 · max 50 750 pts ceiling

Proof: completed pre-survey + post-survey for every participant, plus the submission form. Both forms = one verified interview. One form = zero points.

0
verified

Setup

  • Pre-survey link open and QR ready on your phone
  • Post-survey link ready — it is a different form from the pre-survey
  • Build one notebook in advance with your own course material, not a generic topic
  • Run through all three demos yourself once, end to end
  • Pick a quiet spot, or block a quiet online slot
  • Build a list of 13 names, not 10 — people drop out
  • Skip anyone who already knows Study Notebooks well

Each interview · 15–20 min

  • Greet, confirm they have 10–15 minutes
  • Pre-survey filled by them, before you start talking. You never fill it for them
  • Q1–Q2: surface the pain (how they pick what to study, how they take notes)
  • Q3: the "would you use it" question
  • Q4: introduce Study Notebooks
  • Demo 1 — Create a Notebook with their own material
  • Demo 2 — Diagnostic Quiz, let them see their gaps appear
  • Demo 3 — Progress Dashboard: strengths, focus areas, what next
  • Hand them the device for at least one step
  • Post-survey filled before they leave, with your ambassador GID in it
  • Save both confirmation screenshots / receipts
  • Log it in the portal immediately — do not batch it for the 30th

Full interview script

Opening
Use as a guide, not a script. Talk naturally.
YOU: "Hey! I'm {NAME}, Google Student Ambassador for {CAMPUS}. Got a few minutes? I want to show you something that helps you build full-fledged notes for your exams, without the hours it usually takes. Got 10 minutes?"

(wait for yes)

YOU: "Perfect — before we start, can you fill this quick form? Scan the QR here."
[share pre-survey link — WAIT until they finish]

YOU: "Okay, where are you with exam prep right now? Be honest — on track, a bit behind, or haven't opened the books yet?"
Q1 – Q4
Q1 and Q2 surface the pain. Q3 sums up the need. Q4 is the answer.
Q1  "When your professor shares the syllabus and a pile of PPTs and PDFs, how do you decide what to study first?"

Q2  "How do you make notes right now — handwritten, typed, borrowing a friend's, screenshotting slides? Which part do you hate the most?"

Q3  "If a tool used your own class material to find your weak units and teach those first, would you use it?"

Q4  "That's close to what Study Notebooks does inside Google Gemini. It works from your own PPTs and notes. Let me show you."
Demo 1 — Create a Notebook
Ask what material they have for their next exam, then use exactly that.
"First is Create a Notebook. Go to Google Gemini in your browser, open the menu, click New notebook, then Study and Learn."

"Write your project name and idea — for example: 'Marketing Exam Prep: I want to revise the 4Ps of Marketing and Consumer Behaviour before my exam.'"

"Then upload your class notes or PPTs."

Prompt: "Help me study for my exams using my class notes and slides."

(wait for results) "See how much time this saves you."

Watch for: the moment they recognise their own slide deck being used. That is the story.
Demo 2 — Diagnostic Quiz
This is the moment that changes faces. Let it breathe.
"Now the Diagnostic Quiz. Before your exams, run a quick quiz in the same notebook to spot your syllabus gaps."

Ask: "Honestly — don't you think knowing your gaps first helps you revise better?"
(wait for their answer)

Show how it marks them strong in some units and weak in others — e.g. strong in the 4Ps, weak in market segmentation.
Demo 3 — Progress Dashboard + close 
"And the last one — the Progress Dashboard. After the lesson, it updates your strengths and focus areas."

Show strengths (e.g. Product and Pricing) and top priorities (e.g. Segmentation and Targeting).

Cue: "It tells you what to study next." (wait for their response)

CLOSE: "So — do you think Study Notebooks would help you with your next exams?" (wait)

"One last thing: please fill this post-survey form."
[share post-survey link — WAIT until they finish]

"Study Notebooks has way more to explore while you study. Good luck, and thank you!"

Do / don't

The difference between 10 verified and 10 rejected 
DO
- Show Study Notebooks live on your device, or share your screen
- Let them try a topic from their own semester
- Ask follow-ups when they look confused or curious
- Keep it a conversation, not an interrogation
- Pick a quiet spot, or a stable quiet call

DON'T
- Read the questions robotically
- Fill the forms for them — ever
- Rush it
- Interview students who already know Study Notebooks well
- Count an interview where one form is missing

What voids an entry

These are enforced, not advisory. Violations mean point deductions, event cancellation or disciplinary action — the exact things that make you miss the welcome kit.

  • No recording, or cameras off. If the Meet isn't recorded and faces aren't visible, the session does not count.
  • Your own Meet link. You must use the official link. The team can join any session unannounced and cancel it on the spot.
  • Moving the date or time. The event must run exactly on the submitted slot. Unannounced changes = zero points.
  • Registrations that don't match faces. Your registered list is cross-checked against the recording. Any mismatch invalidates the event.
  • Duplicate, fake or recycled links. Every Gemini link is scanned. One bad link rejects the whole group's entries.
  • Filling forms for participants. Both Study Notebooks forms must be filled by the participant, not you.
  • Missing self-intro. Name, designation, campus and GID, on camera, recorded.
  • Nominating a weak "Best Entry". Nominations are manually reviewed against the criteria.
  • Text or violence in the game poster. No letters, no weapons. Re-roll the image until it's clean.
  • Missing caption elements. GID, all three tags, all three hashtags, plus your region hashtag.
